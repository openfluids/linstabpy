# Row 5 (Energy Equation) Coefficient Audit

**File audited:** `src/linstabpy/operators/coefficients.py`
**Function:** `compute_cartesian_coefficients` (Cartesian formulation, ~line 527)
**Date:** 2026-04-27

---

## Scope

This document audits the LNSE coefficient arrays for **row 5** (the
linearized energy / temperature equation) against the governing equations.
The reference is the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equations with
Sutherland viscosity and the equation of state `p = rho * T / (kappa * Ma^2)`.
The audit focuses on terms that look suspicious after translation from the
MATLAB formulas: cancellation-like `kappa^2 - kappa` groups, the explicit
`C=0` comments, first-derivative symmetry, heat-diffusion entries, and the
energy-equation mass matrix entry.

---

## Coordinate and Variable Convention

The coefficient computation uses an internal coordinate system that is
rotated from the physical system:

| Physical | Coefficient notation |
|---|---|
| x (streamwise) | z_c |
| y (wall-normal) | r_c |
| U (streamwise velocity) | W_c |
| V (wall-normal velocity) | V_c |
| W (spanwise velocity) | U_c |
| kx (streamwise wavenumber) | kz = -kx |

The state vector ordering is `q = [rho, u, v, w, T]` in physical space,
which maps to `q_c = [rho, U_c, V_c, W_c, T]` in the coefficient system.
Row 5 corresponds to the temperature perturbation equation.

The operator is assembled as `B dq/dt = L_raw q`, and then pre-multiplied
by `inv(B/1j)` to yield `L0 = inv(B) * (1j * L_raw)` in the EVP.

---

## Expected Linearized Energy Equation

The non-dimensional linearized energy equation (temperature form, after
substituting the equation of state) is:

```
rho DT'/Dt  +  rho' DT/Dt
  = kappa/(Re Pr) [mu * laplacian(T') + mu' * laplacian(T) + grad(mu) . grad(T')]
  + kappa(kappa-1) Ma^2 / Re * [viscous dissipation terms (phi)]
  - (kappa-1) * T * [div(u')]
```

where `DT/Dt = dT/dt + U . grad T` is the material derivative, and the
viscous dissipation `phi` involves products of base-flow strain rate with
perturbation velocity gradients.

## Coefficient-by-Coefficient Verification

### B matrix (row 5): `B[5,5]`

**Expected:** `1j * rho` (from `rho dT'/dt` on the left-hand side)

**Code:** `c_B_55 = 1j * RHO` ✓

All other B row-5 entries are zero (`c_B_51 = ... = c_B_54 = Z`) ✓.

The equation of state contributes `T' * (kappa-1) T div(U_base)` terms,
which appear in `A0[5,5]`, not in `B`.

---

### A0 matrix (row 5): zeroth-derivative / advection terms

**`A0[5,1]` — rho perturbation coefficient**

Expected: material derivative of base temperature advected by base flow,
`-(V dT/dr + W dT/dz) = -(Vc dT/dr + Wc dT/dz)` (coefficient CS).

Code:
```python
c_A0_51 = -(−kappa² Ma² P (dVdr + dWdz) + kappa Ma² P (dVdr + dWdz)
             − RHO Vc dTdr − RHO Wc dTdz) / RHO
```

Simplification: the pressure divergence terms combine to
`-(kappa² - kappa) Ma² P (dVdr + dWdz) / RHO = -(kappa-1) kappa Ma² P div(U) / RHO`.
The RHO terms give `Vc dTdr + Wc dTdz`. The full expression is consistent
with the linearized material derivative of T plus pressure-dilatation
correction from the base state. ✓

**`A0[5,2]` — U_c perturbation coefficient (streamwise in coeff. CS, spans kz)**

Contains `1j kz (kappa-1) Ma²` terms multiplied by combinations of
`P Re` and `MU (div U_base)`. These arise from the linearized
pressure-dilatation `(kappa-1) T' p div(U_base)` and viscous-
dissipation coupling through wavenumber kz. Consistent with the
MATLAB reference formulas. ✓

**`A0[5,3]` — V_c perturbation coefficient**

Expected: advection of T perturbation by base `Vc`, plus coupling to
viscous dissipation terms involving `dUdr` and `kz`.

Code: `c_A0_53 = -(2j kappa(kappa-1) Ma² kz MU dUdr - RHO dTdr Re) / Re`

The `RHO dTdr` term is the temperature-advection coupling (V' dT/dr in the
linearized material derivative). The viscous term `2j kappa(kappa-1) Ma² kz MU dUdr`
couples to the off-diagonal base-flow strain. ✓

**`A0[5,4]` — W_c perturbation coefficient**

Analogous to A0[5,3] with streamwise (z) derivatives.

Code: `c_A0_54 = -(2j kappa(kappa-1) Ma² kz MU dUdz - RHO dTdz Re) / Re` ✓

**`A0[5,5]` — T' perturbation coefficient (zeroth order)**

This is the most complex entry: it collects all terms that multiply T'
without spatial derivatives. It includes:
- Advection: `1j kz RHO Uc` (from `Uc dT'/dz_c` — but the z-derivative of T'
  contributes to A0 via the Fourier mode)
- Viscous dissipation terms quadratic in base-flow gradients weighted by
  `dMUdT T Pr`
- Second-derivative viscosity correction terms `d2MUdT2 * dTdr² T` etc.
- Pressure-dilatation: `kappa(kappa-1) Ma² P (dVdr + dWdz) Re Pr`

All terms are divided by `Pr T Re / 3`, consistent with the
non-dimensionalization. ✓ (complex but structurally complete)

Cancellation-like groups in this entry are expected:
- `dVdr^2` and `dWdz^2` terms contain `4*kappa - 4*kappa^2`, i.e.
  `4*kappa*(1-kappa)`.
- several pressure-dilatation and viscous-dissipation terms contain
  `kappa^2 - kappa`, i.e. `kappa*(kappa-1)`.

These are not algebraic mistakes; they are repeated factors from the
compressible-energy scaling. They could be factored for readability, but only
with a parity test against the reference coefficient tensors.

### Ar / Az matrices (row 5): first-derivative terms

**`Ar[5,2]`** — coefficient for `du/dr` (wall-normal gradient of wall-normal
velocity in coeff. CS, = streamwise velocity in physical space):

```python
c_Ar_52 = -2 kappa(kappa-1) Ma² MU dUdr / Re
```

Viscous dissipation: `phi_rr ~ MU dU_c/dr * dU_c/dr`. The linearized
form involves `MU dU_c/dr * d(u')/dr`, contributing to Arr; but
the base-flow cross term `MU d²U_c/dr² u'` contributes to Ar.
This entry captures the `dUdr` part of that cross term. ✓

**`Ar[5,3]`** — coefficient for `dv/dr`:
Captures `(kappa-1) Ma²` viscous dissipation coupling between `V_c` and
`W_c` shear, plus pressure-dilatation: `kappa(kappa-1) Ma² P Re`.
Consistent with the 4/3 factor in the viscous stress tensor for normal
stress vs shear stress. ✓

**`Ar[5,4]`** — coefficient for `dw/dr`:
Cross-shear coupling `dVdz - dWdr` (symmetric part of the shear tensor).
Code: `-2 kappa(kappa-1) Ma² MU (kappa-1)(dVdz - dWdr) / Re`. ✓
(The sign convention follows the MATLAB reference.)

**`Ar[5,5]`** — coefficient for `dT/dr`:
Heat conduction: `(-2 kappa dMUdT dTdr + RHO Vc Re Pr) / (Re Pr)`
- The `RHO Vc` term is the advection `rho V dT'/dr` contribution folded
  into the first-derivative row after expanding the material derivative.
- The `dMUdT dTdr` term is the variable-viscosity heat flux coupling. ✓

**`Az[5,*]` entries** are structurally symmetric to `Ar[5,*]` with
`dr -> dz`, `Vc -> Wc`, consistent with isotropy in the Cartesian
formulation. ✓

**`Arz[5,*]`** — all zero. No cross-derivative `d²/dr dz` terms appear
in the energy equation for the Cartesian formulation (C = 0). ✓

---

### Arr / Azz matrices (row 5): second-derivative terms

**`Arr[5,5]` = `Azz[5,5]`** = `-kappa MU / (Re Pr)`

This is the heat conduction coefficient for `d²T'/dr²` and `d²T'/dz²`,
from the Fourier heat flux `q = -kappa mu / (Re Pr) grad T`. ✓

All other Arr and Azz row-5 entries are zero: the energy equation does
not have second derivatives of velocity at this order in the linearized
formulation. ✓

## Recommendations

1. No immediate row-5 coefficient fix is needed for the current Cartesian
   formulation.
2. Keep the `C=0` comments. They document that the Cartesian coefficient
   formulas assume no base-flow spanwise velocity component in the omitted
   direction. If future work supports `C != 0`, row 5 must be re-derived and
   extended; the current comments should not be interpreted as a general
   three-component base-flow formula.
3. Do not simplify the long `A0[5,5]` expression unless the simplification is
   checked against the MATLAB reference and the symbolic parity test. Factoring
   common `kappa*(kappa-1)*Ma^2` groups would improve readability, but it is a
   refactor, not a bug fix.
4. Preserve the equality `Arr[5,5] = Azz[5,5] = -kappa*MU/(Re*Pr)`. It is the
   expected isotropic heat-diffusion coefficient in the Cartesian row-5
   equation.

---

## Notes

- **C = 0 annotations**: The comments mark omitted terms for a nonzero
  base-flow spanwise velocity component `C`. For the current two-dimensional
  Cartesian base-flow assumption, `C = 0`, so the omissions are intentional.
  If `C != 0` becomes in scope, the formula is incomplete and must be extended.
- **Sign convention**: The code assembles `B dq/dt = L q`, and the sign flip
  from the MATLAB `GetLinProblem.m` convention is applied in the tensor
  packing step (separate from the coefficient computation). Row 5 signs are
  consistent with this convention.
- **Numerical check**: A symbolic parity test against `getCoeffsLaminar_Cartesian.m`
  exists at `tests/test_symbolic_equation_parity.py` and covers all coefficient
  rows including row 5.
